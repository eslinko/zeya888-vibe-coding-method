---

## 1. Two audiences and the promise to each

| Audience | Documentation promise |
|----------|------------------------|
| **Technical operator with their own agents** | Open, complete process in the repo: understand → connect → run the cycle without the author on routine work |
| **Fintech vibe-coder** (strong domain mind, little code) | Clear value, roles, process shape; preparation for **guided adoption** with the author |

Both audiences see the same technical rules. The difference is **preparation depth** and **route outcome**, not “secret” files.

---

## 3. What the fintech route teaches

- Why the method exists (discipline between human and agent)  
- Roles: who decides, who executes, what is fixed on disk  
- How discussion differs from plan, materialize, and execute  
- How to describe the project and constraints for onboarding  
- Where safe self-study ends without a false “I’m a solo architect now”

Training: 01 → 02 → 03B.

---

## 4. Outcomes that need guided adoption

- First adaptation of the method to a non-standard stack / org process  
- Resolving ownership, privacy, deploy conflicts in a live fintech context  
- Team role setup (who is operator, who is reviewer)  
- Coaching through the first real waves until steady self-run  
- Calibrating “what can be delegated to agents” to product risk appetite  

Completing dialogues **does not** replace these outcomes.

---

## 5. Typical onboarding / advisory content

1. Review the brief from training 03B (goals, constraints, roles).  
2. Map what already exists in the repo vs what must be created.  
3. Jointly pick a narrow first slice (not “the whole product”).  
4. Coach 1–N waves with process error review (not only code).  
5. Handoff: solo-ops checklist + when to call the author again.

## 7. Honest positioning

- **No** artificial barriers in the open rules: the tech route is sufficient for a competent operator.  
- **No** overstated readiness: the fintech route does not grant a “full solo engineer” certificate.  
- AI-assisted learning relies on the **real** repository documentation, not invented shortcuts.  
- Goal: raise what a person can do with agents; consultation accelerates and reduces risk where experience is missing.
